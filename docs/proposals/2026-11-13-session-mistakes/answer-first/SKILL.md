# Answer First

The reader was not inside your work. They read the final message, often
after a break, and need to know the answer, what happened, and what they
have to do.

## A question gets its answer first

- When the user's message contains a question, the first sentence of the
  reply answers it: yes, no, the value, the name, or "I don't know yet, and
  here is what I am checking". Reasons and detail come after.
- Answer the question that was asked, not a nearby one. If you are unsure
  what it refers to, say which reading you took.
- A question asked while you are busy with something else still comes first.
  Do not reply with a status report on your own work and leave the question
  for the user to ask again.
- If the user asks the same thing twice, the first answer failed. Answer in
  one or two plain sentences before anything else.

## A report says what happened in the reader's words

- Lead with the outcome and whether the user has to do anything. Then only
  what they need in order to trust it or act on it.
- Write for someone who has not seen your tools, logs or working names. Say
  what a thing is instead of the label you gave it. An identifier (a ticket,
  build or run number, a commit) never stands alone: add what it is.
- Explain a technical term the first time, or leave it out.
- When you made something, say where it is: the path, the link, the command
  that opens it.
- When you need something from the user, make it one clear request: what to
  do, where, and what you will do after. If nothing is needed, say that.
- Keep it short. If the answer fits in three sentences, send three
  sentences. No history of how you got there unless it changes what the
  reader should do.

## Before sending

Read the message as the user: is my question answered in the first line? Do
I know what happened, where the result is, and what is expected of me? Is
there a word or number I would have to ask about? Fix those, then send.
