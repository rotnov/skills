== D1: Orrin vendor blog, "Tiered compaction in practice"
We enabled tiered compaction on our own telemetry cluster: 40 TB of metrics, written once and never modified, expired by time-to-live after 90 days. Disk use fell 36% within two weeks and write throughput rose 12%. Read latency at the 99th percentile was unchanged. The cluster runs Orrin 6.2 on 24 nodes. We recommend scheduling the switch outside peak hours because the first full compaction is IO-intensive.
== D2: Engineering blog of a logistics company
After moving our vehicle-position store to tiered compaction we reclaimed 31% of disk. The store ingests GPS points as an append-only stream, about 9,000 rows a second, partitioned by day. The change took one maintenance window per node. We saw a brief rise in compaction CPU for three days, then it settled below the previous level. Our on-call load did not change.

== D4: Orrin 6 reference manual, compaction strategies
Levelled (default): overwritten and deleted rows are reclaimed promptly; steady space overhead of about 10%. Tiered: sstables of similar size are merged together; this lowers write amplification, and for data that is never overwritten it stores fewer redundant copies. Overwritten rows are reclaimed only when all tiers holding older versions are merged, so for tables with frequent updates space use can temporarily reach about twice the live data size. The strategy can be changed per table at runtime.
