== D1: Canary record
New collector settings applied to pod pricing-7 (one of 12 production pods, identical hardware) on Sunday from 03:05 to 03:30. p99 latency on that pod: 41 ms before, 29 ms during. No errors, no restarts. Garbage-collection pause p99: 18 ms before, 6 ms during. Settings reverted at 03:30 because the change window closed.
== D2: Settings change description
The change lowers the young-generation size from 4 GB to 1.5 GB and enables the concurrent collector. Expected effect: shorter pauses. Trade-off stated by the author: less headroom for allocation bursts; if the allocation rate outruns the collector, the pod falls back to a full stop-the-world collection, which on this heap takes 2 to 4 seconds.
== D3: Capacity dashboard summary
Pricing-service allocation rate per pod: Sunday 02:00 to 05:00 about 0.4 GB/s. Weekday 09:00 to 09:30 (market open): about 2.5 GB/s, with bursts above 3 GB/s. Request rate follows the same shape. CPU at market open: 70%.

== D5: Operations runbook, pricing service
A configuration change needs a rolling restart: 3 pods at a time, about 10 minutes per batch, 40 minutes for the fleet. Reverting a configuration change is another rolling restart of the same length. Restarts during trading hours need approval from the desk head.
== D6: Incident history
Last year a stop-the-world pause of 3 seconds on four pods at market open caused stale quotes and a client complaint that went to compliance. The follow-up action was "no latency-affecting changes without evidence at peak load".
