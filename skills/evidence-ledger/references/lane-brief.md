# Lane brief

Read this when you are about to dispatch lanes, or when you work alone and are
about to write your own claim lines.

Fill the template once per lane. Change only the assigned rival. Do not put
your own guess, or anything another lane found, into a brief.

```text
Question: <the question as asked>
Target: <the exact setup or population the decision applies to>
Rivals: A) <...> B) <...> C) <...>
Your job: test rival <X> hardest. Look for what would prove it and for what
would kill it. Report everything you find that bears on any rival, including
facts that hurt <X>.

Report as claim lines, most decision-relevant first, one per fact:
- <fact, with its numbers> | origin: <who first observed or measured it; if
  your source repeats or cites another, name that one> | rung: told /
  documented / observed-elsewhere / observed-on-target | setup: <where it was
  observed, and each difference from the target that could matter>

Then one line: "Open: <what you could not find or check>".
Do not give a recommendation. Do not write "confirmed" or "verified"; give the
rung. If two of your sources share an origin, write one claim line.
```

Why lanes return claim lines and no verdict: a verdict hides the origin, the
rung and the setup, and those are the three things the merge needs. A lane
that sees a third of the evidence cannot know whether its conclusion survives
the rest.
