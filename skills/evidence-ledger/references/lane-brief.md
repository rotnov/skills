# Lane brief

Read this when you are about to dispatch lanes, or when you work alone and are
about to write your own claim lines.

Send every lane the same brief; change only the assigned rival. Do not put
your own guess, or anything another lane found, into a brief.

```text
Question: <the question as asked>
Target: <the exact setup or population the decision applies to>
Rivals: A) <...> B) <...> C) <...>
Test rival <X> hardest, and report every fact that bears on any rival,
including facts that hurt <X>.

You see only part of the evidence; others see the rest. So do not conclude,
recommend, or list what is missing. Report facts as claim lines, most
decision-relevant first:

- <fact, with its numbers> | origin: <who first observed or measured it; if
  your source repeats or cites another, name that one> | how: said /
  stated by a primary source / measured or run | where: <the setup it was
  observed on, in the source's own terms>

One line per origin: sources that repeat each other are one line. Keep the
setup details even when they look unimportant to you: another lane may hold
the fact that makes them matter.
```

Why lanes return claim lines and no verdict: a lane that sees a third of the
evidence is right to call the question open, and a coordinator that merges
three such verdicts inherits three sets of doubts the pooled facts would have
settled. A verdict also hides the origin and the setup, which the merge needs
to tell one source repeated from several, and a result elsewhere from a
result on the target.
