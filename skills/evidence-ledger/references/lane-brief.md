# Lane brief

Read this when you write the lane briefs yourself and want shorter reports.
Skip it when reports already exist.

Send every lane the same brief. Do not put your own guess, or anything another
lane found, into it.

```text
Question: <the question as asked>
Target: <the exact setup or population the decision applies to>
Report every fact that bears on the question, for or against.

You see only part of the evidence; others see the rest. So do not conclude,
recommend, or list what is missing. Report facts as claim lines, most
decision-relevant first:

- <fact, with its numbers> | origin: <who first observed or measured it; if
  your source repeats or cites another, name that one> | how: said /
  stated by a primary source / measured or run | where: <the setup it was
  observed on, in the source's own terms>

One line per origin: sources that repeat each other are one line. Keep the
setup details even when they look unimportant to you: another lane may hold
the fact that makes them matter.
```

What this buys, as measured: reports about a fifth shorter and a cheaper
merge. It did not change which decision the merge reached; step 3 of the skill
does that.
