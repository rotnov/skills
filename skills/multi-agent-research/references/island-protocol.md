# Island Protocol

## Problem coverage

For each relevant formulation, record:

| Fields | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| `problem_id`, `statement` | Stable identity and precise assertion or question. |
| `assumptions`, `domain` | Applicability conditions. |
| `relation_to_goal`, `relation_justification` | Equivalent, sufficient, useful-only, or unknown; evidence for the relation. |
| `open_obligations` | Unproved implications, reductions, or transfer conditions. |
| `coverage` | Assigned islands/tasks, deferred with reason, or excluded by scope. |

Account for every variant supplied by the user or an authoritative in-scope
source. Do not claim exhaustive coverage of an open-ended problem. Consider
constructive and counterexample approaches where meaningful. Three methods for
one narrowed statement do not cover three admissible statements; coverage need
not fund every formulation simultaneously.

## Island charter and local work

A charter records ID/version, investigated problem IDs, method and its difference
from other islands, hypotheses, assigned executors, source baseline, allotted
effort, checkpoint condition, imports, open obligations, and isolation limits.
Keep local experiments, negative findings, and synthesis with that charter.
Replacing a worker transfers this record; it does not erase its history.

Choose the smallest useful population. Three islands with one or two executors
each is an optional starting arrangement, not a fixed topology. Two collaborators
on boundary handling and buffer reuse may belong to one chunking island; a
different representation can define another. Count programs, not people or roles.

Give a speculative or frame-changing method a bounded first exploration pass.
Keep at least one viable alternative from adopting the leader while a meaningful
test remains affordable. Evaluate a cheap decisive probe even without a positive
finding; park a path with a reason rather than treating no progress as refutation.

## Enabling problems

An `enabling_problem` records the parent `problem_id`, changed/relaxed assumptions,
expected reusable output, why it could unblock the parent, bounded allowance,
scope classification, and `transfer_obligations`. Consider an adjacent or smaller
problem when its result could change the main investigation, not only at startup.

Preserve the direction of every implication. For example, success with lossy
compression is useful-only for an exact-reconstruction goal until a valid
transfer is established. A promising enabling result may redirect already
authorized effort; an unrelated project or new access requires authorization.

## Information boundaries and checkpoints

Maintain three layers: common ground (mission, constraints, baseline, verified
corrections), island-local work, and selected migration packets. Before first
checkpoints, send common ground without sibling conclusions. Local collaborators
may exchange within their program; the coordinator can relay scoped messages.

A checkpoint contains a substantive finding or precise negative/blocked result
with a bounded next probe. Freeze each pre-exchange report and provenance, triage
evidence and shared roots, select relevant transfers, record recipient imports
and dispositions, then issue revised briefs for adopted findings. Execute the
bounded follow-up or record it as pending/parked. A polished essay is not an
experiment, and a proposed assignment is not an executed one.

Apply verified material common-ground corrections even to a protected holdout;
record its resulting exposure. Delayed or interrupted islands remain partial or
blocked. Never fill their missing findings with a synthesizer's prediction.
